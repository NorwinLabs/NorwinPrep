Morse code

Hold the screen up as a light signal, or tap the phone's torch in the same pattern. Dot = short, dash = three times as long.

The alphabet

Spelling alphabet (NATO)

Say numbers one digit at a time: 9 = "niner", 5 = "fife", 3 = "tree". Say "I spell" before spelling a word.

Radio frequencies worth knowing

Distress frequencies are for real emergencies only. Most radio services need a licence or are limited by country and power: check the rules where you live before you transmit. This list is a starting point, not legal advice.

FRS / GMRS handheld channels (USA)

FRS needs no licence; GMRS does (it allows more power). Channels 8–14 are FRS only, at low power.

Making a radio call

  1. Listen first, so you do not talk over someone.
  2. Press and hold to talk, wait a second, then speak slowly and clearly.
  3. Say who you are calling, then who you are: "Base, this is Scout."
  4. Keep it short. Say "over" when you finish and expect an answer; "out" when the conversation is done.
  5. In an emergency say "Mayday" three times, then where you are, what is wrong and how many people need help.
  6. Save the battery: turn the radio off between scheduled check-in times (for example, on the hour).
